How to use the Ecommerce Profit calculator

  1. Enter the product cost and your selling price.
  2. Enter shipping cost per unit and the platform and payment gateway fee percentages.
  3. Enter ad spend per unit sold.
  4. Read net profit, net margin, markup and total cost per unit.

Formula

Platform fee = price × platform %; payment fee = price × gateway %

Total cost per unit = product cost + shipping + platform fee + payment fee + ad spend

Net profit = Price − Total cost

Net margin = net profit ÷ price; markup = net profit ÷ product cost.

Worked example

Product cost 500, price 1,500, shipping 150, platform fee 5%, payment fee 3%, ad spend 200 per unit.

  • Platform fee = 75; payment fee = 45
  • Total cost = 970
  • Net profit = 1,500 − 970 = 530
  • Net margin = 35.33%; markup = 106%

Before ads, each unit leaves 730, so that is the most you could spend on ads per unit before the sale makes a loss.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate profit per unit in ecommerce?

Subtract every per-unit cost (product, shipping, marketplace and payment fees, ad spend) from the selling price. The result is net profit per unit.

What costs does this calculator leave out?

Returns, fixed per-transaction fees, taxes and overhead are not modelled unless you fold them into the cost fields you enter. Add an estimate for expected return losses to the shipping or product cost field.

What is the difference between margin and markup?

Margin is profit as a share of the selling price. Markup is profit as a share of cost. A 50% markup equals a 33.3% margin.

How much can I spend on ads per unit?

Take the price and subtract product cost, shipping and fees. Whatever is left is the most you can spend per unit before you lose money. Compare it with your break-even ROAS.

What is a good ecommerce profit margin?

It varies widely by category, volume and fulfilment model, so there is no single target. Use your own cost structure and cash-flow needs to set one.

Can I use this for Amazon, Shopify or Daraz?

Yes. Enter the platform or marketplace fee percentage that applies to you; fee schedules change, so check the platform’s current rates.
