E-Commerce Profitability: Per-Unit Costs & Calculator Limits

Published September 5, 2026 · 6 min read

Strong sales do not automatically mean each sale is profitable. Product cost, shipping, platform and payment fees, advertising, returns, packaging, and other expenses can affect the result. This guide separates the costs a simple per-unit calculator models from costs that need to be estimated separately.

The Costs Most Sellers Forget

  • Payment processing — a provider may charge a percentage, a fixed amount per transaction, or both; check its current fee schedule.
  • Marketplace or platform charges — may be separate from payment processing or bundled, depending on the service.
  • Shipping and packaging — enter the relevant per-unit shipping cost and account separately for packaging if it is not included.
  • Returns and refunds — estimate expected unrecovered shipping, restocking, and write-off costs separately if relevant.
  • Ad spend per order — allocate acquisition cost to the order if you want it reflected in unit economics.

Worked Example: Calculator-Modeled Per-Unit Profit

Selling price: $50
Product cost: $18
Shipping per unit: $5.50
Platform fee: 12% of price = $6.00
Payment fee: 2.9% of price = $1.45
Ad spend per unit: $4.00

Total modeled costs per unit$34.95
Modeled profit per unit$15.05
Modeled net margin30.1%

This example uses the calculator’s listed inputs: product cost, selling price, shipping, platform-fee percentage, payment-fee percentage, and ad spend per unit. It applies the percentage fees to the entered selling price.

Important limitation: the calculator does not separately include fixed per-transaction fees, return/refund allowances, packaging, taxes, overhead, inventory loss, or other costs not entered. Add estimates where possible or track them separately; the tool does not calculate break-even unit volume.

Estimate Return Costs Separately

Returns can affect shipping, resale value, restocking labor, and refund timing, but their cost varies by product and policy. The FinCalcHub Marketing E-Commerce calculator has no return-rate or refund input. Estimate an expected per-order return cost separately if you want it included in a broader unit-economics review.

Marketplace Commission Stacks With Payment Fees

Some platforms charge a marketplace commission and payment processing separately; others bundle or structure fees differently. Check the current fee schedule and transaction statement, and avoid subtracting a cost twice if it is already included in another line.

Common Mistakes

  • Calculating profit as price minus product cost only — ignoring every other cost in the chain.
  • Forgetting to allocate ad spend per order — treating marketing as a sunk cost separate from unit economics.
  • Underestimating return costs — assuming a low return rate has negligible impact.
  • Assuming platform fees are always separate or always bundled — check the provider’s current fee breakdown.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why can sales look profitable while cash results differ?
The unit result depends on all costs and refunds, not revenue minus product cost alone. This calculator models only its listed inputs; returns and other omitted costs need separate estimates.

How much do payment processing fees cost?
Fees vary by provider, region, plan, and transaction. Some include both a percentage and a fixed amount; this calculator accepts a percentage and does not separately model a fixed transaction fee.

Should I factor in returns?
If returns create unrecovered shipping, restocking, refunds, or write-offs, estimate those costs separately. The calculator has no return-rate input.

How do I calculate per-unit profit with this tool?
Subtract entered product cost, shipping, platform-fee percentage, payment-fee percentage, and ad spend per unit from the selling price. Add omitted costs separately for a broader estimate.

Does marketplace commission apply on top of payment fees?
It depends on the provider’s fee structure. Check the current breakdown to see whether charges are separate or bundled.
