How SIP Investment Projections Work: Formula & Example

Published September 2, 2026 · 5 min read

A Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) is really compound interest applied to recurring monthly contributions rather than a single lump sum. Understanding the math behind SIP projections helps you set realistic expectations — and avoid the common mistake of assuming your return rate is guaranteed.

The SIP Formula

M = P × [ ((1 + r)^n − 1) / r ] × (1 + r)

Here M is the projected maturity value, P is the monthly contribution, r is the assumed annual rate divided by 12, and n is the number of monthly periods. The extra (1 + r) models contributions at the beginning of each month, as in the FinCalcHub Marketing SIP Planner. A zero-rate case returns the total contributions.

Worked Example

Scenario: Investing $500/month at an assumed 10% annual return for 15 years.

Total invested: $90,000 ($500 × 180 months)
Projected maturity value: approximately $208,962
Projected growth above contributions: approximately $118,962

Notice that gains actually exceed total contributions here — more than half the final value came from growth, not from money you put in. That's the compounding effect doing the work over 15 years.

SIP vs. Lump Sum: Which Wins?

Neither approach is universally better. The outcome depends on contribution timing, the path of returns, and the investor’s circumstances:

  • Regular-contribution approach: spreads purchases over time, so the number of units bought varies with each purchase price. This does not remove market risk or guarantee a better return.
  • Lump-sum approach: the full amount is invested sooner, so it has more time in the market; the result depends on what prices and returns do after investing.

Regular investing can fit a monthly income pattern. The FinCalcHub Marketing planner models a flat monthly contribution; it does not compare funds, model market volatility, or select between SIP and lump-sum investments.

What Is a Step-Up SIP?

A step-up SIP increases the contribution periodically, often annually. This article describes the concept, but the FinCalcHub Marketing planner assumes the same monthly contribution for the full period and does not model step-ups.

The Assumption That Breaks Most Projections

A SIP projection depends on both the contribution schedule and the assumed return rate; the rate is never guaranteed. Real market returns fluctuate year to year; a 10% average over 15 years might include several down years mixed with strong ones. Treat any SIP projection as a planning estimate based on an assumption, not a promise of what you'll actually receive.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a SIP?
A Systematic Investment Plan — investing a fixed amount at regular intervals, usually monthly, rather than all at once.

Is SIP better than lump sum investing?
Neither is universally better. Regular contributions spread purchase timing; a lump sum is invested sooner. Outcomes depend on the actual market path and the investor’s circumstances.

What return rate should I use to project my SIP?
Choose a scenario appropriate to the asset and time horizon, and test more than one assumption. The tool does not recommend a rate, and no assumed return is guaranteed.

What is a step-up SIP?
A plan where contributions increase periodically. The FinCalcHub Marketing SIP Planner models a constant monthly amount rather than a step-up schedule.

Does SIP guarantee returns?
No — it's an investing method, not a guaranteed-return product. Actual returns depend on the underlying fund's real performance.
