How Cryptocurrency Price Conversion Works

Published September 5, 2026 · 4 min read

A crypto-to-fiat result is a multiplication: the crypto amount times a reference price. In FinCalcHub Marketing, the page requests reference-price data through its same-origin endpoint; a reference value can differ from an exchange’s tradable quote and may be cached or a configured fallback.

Where This Converter Gets Price Data

The browser requests the same-origin /api/crypto-prices endpoint. The Vercel function at /api/crypto-prices.js requests Bitcoin, Ethereum, and Solana reference prices from CoinGecko in supported fiat quote currencies. It does not submit a trade or guarantee an exchange execution price.

The Conversion Formula

Fiat Value = Crypto Amount × Reference Price per Unit

The arithmetic is straightforward; the important questions are where the quote came from, whether it is current or cached, and whether a configured fallback is being displayed.

Worked Example (Hypothetical)

Amount: 0.5 BTC
Assumed reference price for this arithmetic example only: $100,000 per BTC

Fiat value = 0.5 × $100,000 = $50,000

The $100,000 figure is hypothetical, not a current FinCalcHub Marketing quote or an exchange price.

Why the Displayed Price Isn't Always What You'd Pay

A converter shows a reference rate. If you actually execute a trade on a specific exchange, you're subject to that exchange's:

  • Order book spread — the gap between the highest buy offer and lowest sell offer at that moment
  • Trading fees — typically a small percentage charged by the exchange
  • Slippage — for larger trades, the price can move as your order fills across multiple price levels

None of this means the reference price is "wrong" — it's simply a representative average, not a guaranteed execution price at any single venue.

Why Crypto Prices Move Faster Than Fiat Exchange Rates

Cryptocurrency markets trade continuously, 24/7, with no centralized closing time, and many have thinner overall liquidity than major fiat currency pairs. This combination means relatively smaller trading volumes can move the price more sharply than you'd typically see in traditional currency markets.

Cached and Fallback Rates

While the page is active, it attempts to refresh prices about every 30 seconds. A successful response is stored in localStorage. On page initialization, the browser accepts a cached record as recent only if its timestamp is less than one hour old; older records may remain stored but are not treated as recent. The endpoint may also serve a recent successful response from a warm function instance for up to five minutes; that response is marked as cached and retains its original timestamp.

If there is no current response or acceptable browser cache, the page can show configured illustrative USD fallback amounts (currently $104,000 per BTC, $2,500 per ETH, and $170 per SOL in this project version). These are not live quotes. Missing fiat-currency values may be estimated using preset conversion factors. The interface labels non-live data as Cached or Fallback; configured fallback values have no market-data timestamp. Verify a current market rate independently before making a decision.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where do FinCalcHub Marketing crypto prices come from?
The page requests its same-origin /api/crypto-prices endpoint, whose Vercel function retrieves reference-price data from CoinGecko.

Why can the displayed price differ from an exchange quote?
A reference price is not a specific exchange’s order-book quote and does not include that exchange’s spread, fees, or slippage.

How often does the converter refresh?
While the page is active, the browser attempts a refresh about every 30 seconds. Network, cache, or fallback status can affect which value is displayed.

What does “cached” or “fallback” mean?
A recent successful value may be read from localStorage; on initialization it is accepted only if its timestamp is less than one hour old. If no acceptable cache or current data is available, the page may use configured illustrative fallback values that are not current market quotes.

Is the converter’s result an execution price?
No. It is a reference or fallback value, not an executable quote. Check the intended exchange’s current quote, spread, and fees before trading.
